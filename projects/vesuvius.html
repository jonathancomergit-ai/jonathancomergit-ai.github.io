The Vesuvius Challenge

In 79 AD, Mount Vesuvius buried a whole library of papyrus scrolls. They’re lumps of carbon now (try to open one and it falls apart). So how do you read a book you can’t open? My team, Team Limitless, is working on it!

A library you can’t open

When the volcano went off, a villa full of scrolls got buried in hot ash. The heat turned every scroll into something like a charcoal log. They survived for almost 2,000 years that way, but the layers are fused together, so unrolling one by hand just crumbles it.

The Vesuvius Challenge is an open competition to read them anyway, without ever touching the inside. People have already pulled real Greek words out of closed scrolls this way, which still blows my mind!

How you read a closed scroll

  • Scan. CT X-rays slice the closed scroll, a few micrometres apart. That gives you a 3D picture of every layer inside.
  • Unroll. You trace one sheet through thousands of those slices and flatten it out on the computer.
  • Find the ink. This is the hard part. The ink is carbon and so is the papyrus, so your eye sees nothing. A model learns the tiny difference in texture where ink sits on the surface.

Checking our tools on ink we already know

Before I trust any tool on a scroll nobody has read, I want to see it find ink that experts have ALREADY found. So we run our whole pipeline on public scrolls that have been read, and compare what comes out against the answer.

KNOWN INK Both pictures are from public scrolls that have already been read. They are not new finds. We use them to prove our pipeline can see real ink before we trust it anywhere else. Our work on unread scrolls stays private while the prizes are open.

Where it is now

HEATSINK

A pixel-art game about building computers. You wire circuit boards from real parts, power them up, and try to survive the heat! It starts with one CPU on one board and ends with a whole datacenter floor that you designed.

Heat is the whole game

Every part makes heat, and heat spreads across the board one tile at a time. A CPU sips 25 watts at normal speed. But crank its clock up and the power climbs with the CUBE of the speed, so double the speed is eight times the heat!

So what happens when it gets too hot? At 85 degrees it slows itself down. At 100 it shuts off. And at 112 it melts for good (and dumps all that heat onto whatever is sitting next to it).

So many ways to stay cool

You start with a plain heatsink and a fan. Then come heat pipes, water blocks, and full custom loops with a pump, a reservoir and radiators. Later on you can sink the whole board in a tank of liquid (the fans inside stop working, so you have to plan for that).

The numbers are tuned to feel real. The best air cooling is built for roughly 175 to 250 watts, a big custom loop for about 1,500, and a boiling immersion tank for 2,000 and up.

From one board to a whole floor

Once a board works, you save it as a blueprint and it becomes a server. Then you fill racks with them on a top-down floor. You run power from a breaker panel and network from a router, and you cool the ROOM now, not just the chip.

There are five spaces to grow into, from a corner of a garage up to your own datacenter. And the floor fights back. Push a power line past 100% for four seconds and it trips, and everything on it goes dark!

The goal is a law of physics

There’s no final boss here, there’s a wall. Physics sets a hard limit on how little energy a computer can use to do its work, and your meter starts ten billion times above it. The whole run is about chasing that number down. It’s one long run with no resets, and it takes about 10 to 15 hours.

Where it is now

The board, the floor and the wall are all built, and I’m playtesting. There’s no Steam page or release date yet, and HEATSINK is still a working title. I’ll post in the dev log when that changes!
